# Slop tells

**rxz** · the diagnostic catalogue.

A tell is not a style you dislike. It is a pattern that makes a viewer read "nobody art-directed this" before they
read the game. Any two of these on one thumbnail is a re-concept, not a polish pass.

The reference galleries of the paid Roblox generators converge on the opposite of this list: one hero filling
more than half the frame, one prop, a hard key light with a rim, one dominant hue, and either no text or a single
word — often *inside* the scene as a sign rather than pasted on top. That is the target.

---

## 1. Composition

| Tell | Why it reads as slop | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Three or more focal characters | At tile size the eye finds no entry point and gives up | One hero, or two in a Versus diagonal |
| Subject dead centre, background equally sharp | No hierarchy; reads as a screenshot | Subject in a third, background blurred or darkened |
| Horizon or strong line through the head | Amateur framing; decapitates the hero | Raise or lower the camera, or move the subject |
| Subject touching or cropped by the edge | Looks accidental, not composed | Keep 6% clear on all sides |
| Empty middle, objects hugging corners | The eye lands on nothing | Fill the centre with the hero's mass |
| Everything in focus | Phone snapshot, not art | Shallow depth of field behind the subject |
| Collage of separate images | Reads as a stolen mood board | One scene, rendered once |

**Test:** squint until detail disappears. You should see one shape and one accent. If you see a texture, the
composition is a mess.

---

## 2. Character

| Tell | Why it reads as slop | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Default blocky avatar, neutral face | The universal "I did not make art" signal | Use the user's own character, posed and emoting |
| The same face twice | Instantly uncanny; reads as a copy-paste | One character, or two visibly different ones |
| T-pose, idle stance, arms at sides | No action, no promise | Mid-motion pose with weight on one leg |
| Neutral expression | Wastes the strongest click driver you have | One emotion, pushed: shock, greed, terror, glee |
| Anime face on a blocky body | Two rendering languages in one figure | Commit to one style anchor for the whole frame |
| Floating limbs, extra fingers, merged props | Model artefacts at the exact place the eye goes | Regenerate; do not "fix in post" a hand |
| Character facing away, no face | Removes the click driver for no gain | Turn the head to camera, or make the object the hero |
| Fake characters from other games | Legal risk, and players recognise the bait | The user's own cast, or a described original |

**Test:** shrink the face to 40 px. Can you still name the emotion? If not, the pose and expression are too small
or too mild.

---

## 3. Light and colour

| Tell | Why it reads as slop | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Flat ambient light | No form, no depth; the classic "pasted on" look | One hard key + one coloured rim |
| No contact shadow | The subject floats | Ground shadow, even a soft one |
| Six or more saturated hues | Rainbow mush at tile size | One dominant hue, one accent, skin/warm as the third |
| Neon glow over everything | Hides the subject, dates instantly | Glow only on the accent |
| Subject and background the same value | The silhouette dissolves | Separate by value, not just by colour |
| Grey or beige overall | Invisible in a saturated feed | Commit to a hue |
| Lens flare across the face | Obscures the one thing that sells | Flare behind, never over |

**Test:** convert to greyscale (`thumb.py compose` writes a grey proof). If the subject vanishes, the lighting is
the problem, not the palette.

---

## 4. Type

| Tell | Why it reads as slop | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Letters generated by the image model | Misspelled, doubled, melting | Never generate text; composite it |
| Four or more words | Nobody reads a sentence at 256 px | One to three, or none |
| Thin or decorative font | Disappears when shrunk | Anton / heavy condensed, high contrast |
| Text in the bottom-right corner | Platform chrome lands there on several surfaces | Lower-left, or top-left |
| Text repeating the game name | The name is already under the tile | Say something the title cannot |
| Outline so thick it closes counters | Reads as a sticker, not a title | Stroke ≈ 6–9% of cap height |
| Gradient/rainbow text | Fights the art, dates the thumbnail | One flat colour, one accent word |
| Text centred over the hero's face | Covers the click driver | Put type beside the subject, not on it |

**Test:** the 256 px proof. If you have to lean in, it is too small or too long.

---

## 5. Promise

| Tell | Why it reads as slop | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Shows something the game does not do | One click, one dislike, worse ranking | Show the first-minute loop |
| Fake rewards ("+9999", "FREE") | Reads as a scam ad, not a game | Real numbers, or none |
| Borrowed anime characters | Players know it is bait | Original cast, or the actual game |
| A "STEAL A ___" clone composition | The most saturated look in the feed | Differentiate on the promise, not the template |
| Lore or map beauty shot | Beautiful, says nothing about play | The loop, not the location |
| Ten thumbnails, one image | The gallery looks automated | One archetype per slot |

**Test:** show the thumbnail and the game's first minute to someone who has played neither. Can they match them?

---

## 6. Production and crop

| Tell | Why it reads as slop | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Anything important in the outer 6% | Cropped on some surfaces | Keep the safe zone clear |
| Text or a face in the bottom-right | Overlaid by platform chrome | Reserve it |
| Upscaled from 512 px | Soft everywhere, worst on the face | Generate at 2K, or at least 1920 wide |
| Visible JPEG mush around edges | Re-encoded twice | Upload a clean PNG |
| Different aspect than 16:9 | Cropped or letterboxed by the platform | 1920×1080 exactly |

---

## 7. Set-level tells

- **Same image, ten colourways.** The gallery reads as one asset, so the game looks small.
- **Every slot is a hero shot.** No gameplay proof, no reward, no "what just happened".
- **The first slot is the prettiest, not the clearest.** Slot one is the ad; it must communicate fastest.
- **No refresh.** A thumbnail that has not changed in months is a thumbnail nobody is testing.

---

## Where these rules come from

Reviewed October 2026, public galleries only (both generators' actual tools are behind a login): RoThumbs'
four example thumbnails — a horror two-shot with a looming antlered threat, a tycoon character with a diegetic
"SOLD" sign, a pet key and diamond shot top-down like a product, and a jester mid-spike — and RoVisuals'
before/after example, which shows the same scene before and after adding expression, motion, a prop and an arrow.

The strongest examples share four things, and every one of them is a rule above: one hero filling more than half
the frame, one prop, one hard key with a rim, and either no text or a single word. The "before" halves are the
catalogue: flat light, neutral pose, no focal point, no promise.

---

## The two-minute audit

1. Open the 256 px proof at actual size. Name the promise out loud.
2. Greyscale. Does the hero survive?
3. Count focal subjects. More than two? Re-concept.
4. Read the title. More than three words? Cut.
5. Cover the title. Does the image still sell the game?
6. Look at the edges. Anything you need, touching the frame?
7. Name one thing in the image that could only belong to *this* game. If you cannot, it is generic.
